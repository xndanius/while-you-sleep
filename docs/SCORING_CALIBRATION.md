# Calibrating a proposed idea

This reading aid clarifies the existing demand-first contract. RULES.md and the run's frozen scoring policy remain authoritative. It grants no score or approval, changes no weights or threshold, and does not rescore historical records. Legacy product examples still require the evidence appropriate to claims about an existing product.

## Exact score bands from RULES.md

<!-- rubric-bands:start -->
Use modest ratings when support is limited. For ease and feasibility, 1–3 means substantial unresolved skills, access or scope; 4–6 means a plausible small experiment with stated obstacles; 7–8 needs a concrete accessible first-day plan; 9–10 needs strong evidence that this small version is unusually straightforward. Profit 1–3 means an unclear buyer or problem; 4–6 is a plausible buyer hypothesis without validated demand; 7–8 needs evidence of the problem and relevant real use; 9–10 requires unusually strong attributed demand evidence and still promises no earnings. Autonomy 1–3 is mainly manual; 4–6 automates some repeatable steps; 7–8 has a concrete bounded operational loop and explicit human limits; 9–10 needs evidence that nearly all that particular loop runs itself. For legacy product examples, Evidence 1–3 is a description without a working demonstration; 4–6 is first-party working-product or demo evidence; 7–8 adds concrete real-use evidence; 9–10 needs strong, specific and verifiable use evidence. For demand-first proposals, Evidence 1–3 is a vague role/category without a bounded chore; 4–6 is a fresh primary notice with a literal concrete task but limited frequency, inputs or constraints; 7–8 needs detailed task evidence, a credible repeated need and relevant corroboration; 9–10 needs unusually strong, specific, independently corroborated demand and workflow evidence. A paid vacancy is evidence of offered human work, not proof of a paid AI customer. Do not reject a demand-first proposal solely because no AI product exists yet; do reject an invented chore, unsupported business claim or plan whose risks and practical limits make the proposed scope implausible. Absence of customer or revenue evidence must never be filled by imagination.

## Apply the bands to the claim being made

- Rate the declared output, inputs, steps and human boundary. For each limiting reason, identify either a dependency inside that scope or a limitation in the evidence for the problem and customer hypothesis. An excluded production feature is not a missing dependency of the smaller proposal.
- Missing execution, sales or a live integration alone imposes no universal ceiling on a demand-first idea. A required but unsupported capability, unrealistic effort or unclear useful output can still justify a low rating or rejection. A proposed plan or a documentation page supplies no automatic passing score.
- Keep the modest scope honest: if people still upload, review or submit records, do not award autonomy or business value for those manual steps. Do not shrink the plan on paper while promising a full unattended service in the post.
- Evidence for a proposal measures the documented chore, context and relevant support. Missing corroboration can limit it; absence of a verified version of the proposed service is not itself contrary evidence. Profit remains a hypothesis, with stronger bands requiring the evidence specified above, not invented customers or revenue.
- First choose the applicable band, then the justified value within it. Do not require the 9–10 condition to permit a 7–8 rating. Explain actual limits instead of mechanically upgrading a candidate because it has a plan.
- A factual claim that a test, integration, deployment or sale happened still needs independent evidence. Reject false or unsupported claims, including a proposed acceptance check presented as a passed test. Literal source quotes, freshness, privacy, novelty and independent approval remain mandatory.
